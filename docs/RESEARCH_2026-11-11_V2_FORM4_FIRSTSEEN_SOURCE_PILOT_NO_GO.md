# November 11, 2026 — V2 distinct official SEC Form 4 open-market insider purchase pilot (RESEARCH ONLY / NO GO)

## Thesis and scope
Test a **non-price-origin** hypothesis—meaningful, independently verifiable insider purchases disclosed on SEC Form 4—rather than revisiting retired price-chase, GLD lag, one-minute shakeout, sector reversal, or opening-gap strategies. This run investigated whether filing records can support a *causal* intraday entry hypothesis. It **did not** test profitability or place orders.

## Verified official sources
- Official SEC API documentation confirms public company submissions JSON and filing histories, and notes that API indexing is near real time: https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces
- Official SEC Form 4 filing index supplies the accession and **accepted** timestamp; the Form 4 XML supplies actual transaction code, ownership nature, issuer CIK, purchase size and price, officer/director relationship, and any accompanying sale. **Accepted != original observed by our collector**.
- Example of a misleading simple `transactionCode=P`: official filing accession `0001193125-26-455960` for issuer CIK `1325702`, accepted `2026-11-11T01:30:04Z`, contains **both P and S** and **indirectly held stock**. A naive P-only BUY classifier would misleadingly label this as a standalone direct-company insider conviction signal.
- Another real schema trap: SEC Form 4 relationship flags appear as `1`/`0` in some XML (not just `true`/`false`). Corrected the isolated parser and added tests before drawing pilot conclusions. No published results rely on the buggy initial parser.

## Bounded pilot
Used **GET-only** official SEC ticker mapping, issuer submissions history, and the **three most recent original Form 4 XML filings** per issuer, subject to the `2026-11-11T13:40Z` cutoff. The 17 chosen symbols were AMD, MU, NVDA, CAT, GS, JPM, BAC, CRWD, PANW, MSFT, XOM, CVX, UNH, LLY, PEP, ACN, and SBUX. Requests were rate limited. **This is not a census** of all Form 4 transactions, all historic filings, or all stocks; raw submission-array metadata counts cover variable older ranges and are not a 52-week trading denominator.

- **45** actual Form 4 XML filings successfully screened from **15** issuers; `JPM` had an unusable issuer submissions JSON response on this read and `XOM` had no original Form 4 entries in its examined issuer-recent array. Both are **incomplete/unknown coverage**, not proof of no insider purchases.
- **One** bounded filing (CAT) met the strict `>= $250,000` direct-common-stock open-market P preliminary economic filter when temporarily ignoring original first-observed timestamp. Even this is NOT a tradable or historically verified signal.
- **45/45** historical filings lack our contemporaneous original collector `first_observed_at`. **Zero** pass the complete causal research screen; **zero** were broker orders or tested against executable SIP bid/ask/fees.
- Among the 45, the following *overlapping* reasons occurred: **19** same-filing sales, **17** affirmed 10b5-1 plans, **10** derivative transactions, **10** compensation/withholding transactions, **2** issuer CIK/symbol mismatches. These are **data-screening issues**, not measured trade false positives. Many can be legitimate transactions but are intentionally excluded from the proposed high-specificity hypothesis.
- SEC Form 4 metadata may include filers and issuer identity divergences; CIK must be checked against the XML and accepted-time data, rather than treating an issuer-feed row as sufficient proof for symbol execution.
- A filing discovered only after the historical trading decision would be hindsight leakage. The SEC `acceptanceDateTime` is a necessary lower bound but does not establish when our observer saw the filing or original content version. The pilot's 2026 evidence cannot retroactively reconstruct an immutable contemporaneous feed.
